Sunday — Verem, Aguada, then Panjim

Starting mid-afternoon from Candolim. One fort with a museum, sunset on the sand, Goan dinner in Panjim, then the floating casinos on the Mandovi. About 50 km total.

Sunday closures — two things moved

Mae de Deus, Saligao is shut all day Sunday. It's moved to Tuesday morning, when it's open and sits fifteen minutes from you.

Reis Magos Church is also shut Sundays, but the fort beside it is open and is the better half anyway. You get the church exterior, which is the photogenic part.

15:10

Fort and church · closes 17:00

Reis MagosVerem · fort 9:30–17:00 · ₹50 entry

The best-restored fort in Goa and far quieter than Aguada. Built 1551 over the Mandovi, later a prison, now a heritage centre with a permanent Mario Miranda exhibition and cells that held Goan freedom fighters.

The church next door dates to 1555, dedicated to the Three Wise Men, with stone lions on the entrance steps. Interior is closed today; the white façade and the river view are the reason to walk over.

There's a steep climb to the gate. A van runs up for ₹20 a head — worth it. Coming down is about 150 stairs either way, slippery in rain.

9:00

Temple 1

Shri MangeshiMardol, Priol · 5:30–21:30

The most important temple in Goa, dedicated to Shiva, moved here in the 1500s to escape the Portuguese and rebuilt grandly by the Peshwas. The seven-storey white lamp tower in the courtyard is the image everyone knows.

Set against a green hill — in monsoon the whole valley behind it is startling.

Private white-plate cars get the near car park; yellow-plate taxis are sent to the far lot with a long walk. Shoes off, shoulders and knees covered.

Temple 2

Shri ShantadurgaKavlem, Ponda · 6:00–20:30

Higher-rated than Mangeshi and, to many people, the more beautiful of the two. 450 years old, gifted to devotees by Bajirao Peshwa in 1738, at the foot of a hill with a six-tier lamp tower and a temple tank at the gate.

Forty-five minutes covers it. The pond by the entrance is the photo.

Churches · UNESCO

Old GoaBasilica 9:00–17:00 · Se Cathedral 7:30–18:00

Two of them, three minutes apart across one road. The Basilica of Bom Jesus holds the relics of St Francis Xavier. Se Cathedral is the larger — one of the biggest churches in Asia, built to mark the Portuguese capture of Goa in 1510, with the Golden Bell in its tower.

Both fully indoors, so this is the day's wet-weather insurance. Free entry, ₹10 for the art gallery, big free car park.

Dress code enforced at the Basilica on everyone. No shorts, shoulders covered.

11:00

Logistics

Pack and check outmost Goa hotels check out at 11:00 or 12:00

Confirm your checkout time tonight, not tomorrow morning. If it's 11:00 and you leave at 14:30, ask now whether they'll hold the bags — most will, free.

Return the Innova with a full tank if that's the rental terms, and photograph the car and odometer before handing over the keys.

14:30

Depart

Leave for the airportcheck which one — Goa has two

Mopa / Manohar (GOX) is roughly 45 km north of Candolim, about 1 hour 15. Dabolim (GOI) is roughly 40 km south, about 1 hour 15, and the Panjim stretch is worse.

Add 45 minutes to either in monsoon rain. Leaving at 14:30 gets you to the terminal around 15:45 — comfortable for a flight from 17:45 onward, tight for anything earlier.

If 14:30 is your flight time rather than your departure time, you need to leave Candolim by 11:30, which means checkout done by 11:00 and no Mae de Deus.

Before you fly

  • Feni cannot go in hand baggage. Check it in, wrapped, or it gets binned at security.
  • Both airports are slow on a weekday afternoon. Two hours before departure domestic, three if you're checking bags for an international connection.
  • Cabs from Candolim to Mopa run ₹2,500–3,500. Book the night before — app availability on this route is patchy and surge is brutal.
