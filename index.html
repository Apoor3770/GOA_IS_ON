Read this first

The beaches are for walking, not swimming. Water sports are suspended for the monsoon and lifeguards are restricting entry into the sea. Do not go in, especially after drinking — this is the accident that happens every monsoon.

This version is more weather-exposed than the last one. Old Goa and Fontainhas were the rain-proof stops, and they no longer fit around a 12:30 lunch in Anjuna. If it settles into heavy rain, the swap list at the bottom gets you indoors.

The order

North first while it's cool and empty, then work south down the coast. Everything after lunch sits within ten minutes of Anjuna.

Beach 1

AshwemMandrem · widest and cleanest

The quietest beach on this list and the one worth the extra driving. Long flat sand, barely any hawkers, mostly a foreign crowd. Empty at this hour in September.

Few shacks are open this month, so don't count on food here — you've just eaten anyway.

Beach 2

MorjimChapora river mouth

Enormous open stretch where the Chapora meets the sea, and an olive ridley turtle nesting site. Shallow, wide, good for photos with the storm light behind you.

The river-mouth end has strong pull even when it looks flat. Stay on the sand.

Lunch

Burger FactoryAnjuna · 12:30–22:30 · shut Wednesdays

Smashed patties, fresh buns, crispy potato sides thrown in free. Consistently rated the best burger in the state and priced like a normal restaurant, not a beach club.

Portions are huge — regulars say one burger comfortably feeds two. Order four between five and add sides.

The turn off the Anjuna–Mapusa road is easy to miss and the approach is tight. Follow the pin, not the signboard.

Beach 3

Anjunarocky, dramatic in the rain

Black rock outcrops at both ends and heavy monsoon surf breaking over them. Less pretty than Ashwem, far more dramatic right now — this is the one that photographs well when the sky is grey.

Walk the southern rocks toward the flea market end, then come back up for a coffee at one of the cliff cafes.

Fort · closes 17:00 sharp

Chapora FortVagator · 10:00–17:00

Ruined hilltop fort with a 360° view over Vagator, the river mouth and everywhere you've just been. Ten to fifteen minutes up from the car park.

Nothing sold at the top, so carry water. Wet laterite is genuinely slick and the drops are unwalled.

Beach 4

OzranLittle Vagator · under the red cliffs

Small cove tucked below the Vagator cliffs, with the carved Shiva face in the rock at the water's edge. The steep path down is exactly why it stays empty.

That path is loose rock and gets treacherous wet. Public access is via the steps near Bo Tai — the resorts gate their own entrances.

Sundowner into dinner · 3 hrs, one table

PiscoAnjuna · 12:30–00:00 · sunset 18:35

Sea-level terrace right on Anjuna beach, and the seating is laid out so nearly every table faces the water. Get there before sunset and just stay — the evening flows straight from drinks into dinner without moving.

Order: baby rotisserie chicken, calamari, truffle fat chips, the quesadillas.

It's expensive, and reviewers report a cover charge at the door that isn't explained upfront. Ask what it is when you book, and book — five people at sunset will not walk in.

Night

Tito's LaneBaga · peaks after 23:00

Titos, Cafe Mambo and Las Olas sit on one short lane, so you can move between them and bail when it stops working. Mambo has the better crowd, Las Olas is calmer and opens onto the beach.

Cover for men runs roughly ₹1,500–2,000 on a Saturday. Usually free entry for women.

Leave the Innova at your stay. There are breath tests on this stretch and no parking worth the name after eleven.

Driving notes

  • Siolim bridge is the only crossing between Morjim and Anjuna. Don't let Maps talk you into the ferry in monsoon.
  • Ashwem and Morjim have easy roadside parking. Anjuna and Ozran do not — go early or park up on the road and walk down.
  • Chapora — park at the base of the hill, not in the village lanes.
  • Baga at night — cab in, cab out. Don't attempt it in the Innova.
  • Rain — the Anjuna and Assagao inner roads flood in patches and potholes vanish under water. Slow after dark.

If the weather turns

  • Swap the two northern beaches for Old Goa. Basilica of Bom Jesus and Se Cathedral are fully indoors and open 9–5. Adds about 90 minutes of driving but nothing on the list gets rained off.
  • Swap Ozran for Fontainhas. The Portuguese quarter in Panjim looks better wet than dry. Park at the Old Secretariat — the lanes won't take the Innova.
  • Drop the coast entirely. Dudhsagar is at full flow right now and monsoon is the only time it looks like that. Jeep safaris shut in heavy rain, so call before committing to the drive.
Times are targets, not a schedule. Book Pisco and check Burger Factory is open before you drive out. Sunset is 18:35.
